I'll start with clouds, those soft white cakes of air.<br />
I've always thought they'd taste of Sunday morning:<br />
of milk-warm light, of pillows after rest,<br />
silver folded softly around a hundred feathered nests.


Then I'll take the sea.<br />
I'll need a giant spoon, a napkin wide enough to borrow from the moon,<br />
and someone to check my pocket, please before an octopus makes off with my keys.


Then the hills. The mountains sugared white,<br />
the snowy, solemn peaks I'm saving for last.<br />
I'll crack their ridges open and watch the startled goats ride frosting down their backs.<br />
But why, I wonder, are certain things so peculiar to the tongue?


Some taste of rust and thunder.<br />
Some are sour, sharp, and wrong.<br />
Some sting like bees in hiding.<br />
Some bruise, some crawl, some cling.<br />
Some taste of old tin coins and bells<br />
that have forgotten how to ring.


And still, and still, my stomach rumbles louder.<br />
The more I taste, the more I ache<br />
to taste what I have not.<br />
I want the bitter and the sugared,<br />
the spoiled and the sweet,<br />
the splendid, strange, unfinished world.<br />
I have yet to eat.


Ever and ever hungrier,<br />
I want to gobble the wild world whole,<br />
bowl by bowl by bowl.<br />
And when I'm finally full, if full is what I'll be<br />
I'll rise up from the table,<br />
dust the crumbs from my knees,<br />
and cook the world anew for other mouths to share.
